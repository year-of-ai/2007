## Summary

On October 10, 2007, Radiohead released their seventh studio album *In Rainbows* as a digital download with an innovative pay-what-you-want pricing strategy. Following the expiration of their EMI contract after the 2003 album *Hail to the Thief*, the Oxford-based band recorded the album between February 2005 and June 2007 with longtime producer Nigel Godrich at studios including Halswell House in Somerset and their own home-built facility in Abingdon, Oxfordshire. The 42-minute, 10-track release — comprising songs including "Bodysnatchers," "Nude," "Reckoner," and "House of Cards" — allowed fans to set any price, including zero, during its initial two-month digital window. This direct-to-consumer approach bypassed traditional record labels entirely, with orders processed through the band's W.A.S.T.E. management operation.

### Announcement and digital release

The album's existence was announced without warning on October 1, 2007, just nine days before release, in a blog post by guitarist Jonny Greenwood. The abrupt announcement — with no press campaign, no advance singles, and no label intermediary — was itself a statement of the band's new commercial approach. The radiohead.com site experienced heavy traffic on announcement day as fans rushed to register. Comscore, a digital analytics firm, tracked approximately 1.2 million copies downloaded within the first 29 hours of availability. A simultaneous premium "Discbox" package priced at £40 (approximately $80 USD) included two vinyl records, two CDs, a hardbound book of artwork, and additional bonus tracks, and sold out its initial pressing.

### Commercial and critical performance

The digital campaign generated an estimated $3 million in direct revenue from fans who chose to pay, with studies showing approximately 62 percent of downloaders chose a free download while those who paid averaged around $2.26 globally (approximately $6 in the United States and $4.64 in the United Kingdom). Following a retail release on December 3, 2007 (international) and January 1, 2008 (North America) through TBD Records and XL Recordings, the album debuted at No. 1 on both the UK Albums Chart and the US Billboard 200, the latter selling 122,000 copies in its first week of physical retail. It eventually sold over three million copies worldwide by October 2008. Critically acclaimed, *In Rainbows* won Grammy Awards for Best Alternative Music Album and Best Boxed/Special Limited Edition Package at the 2009 Grammy ceremony, and was nominated for Album of the Year. *Rolling Stone* later ranked it among the greatest albums of the 21st century.

## Significance

*In Rainbows* marked a watershed moment in music distribution and artist independence. As *Time* magazine noted, it was "easily the most important release in the recent history of the music business." The pay-what-you-want model, novel for a major established act with a global fanbase, demonstrated that musicians could retain creative and economic control by bypassing traditional record-label gatekeepers and selling directly to fans. The album's commercial success — despite allowing free downloads — proved the viability of alternative distribution models at a time when music industry revenues from physical sales had been declining for six consecutive years.

### Broader industry impact

The release influenced subsequent negotiations between artists and labels across the industry, shifting leverage toward artists in an era when digital piracy had already undermined labels' revenue models. Several artists — including Nine Inch Nails and later Beyoncé and Taylor Swift — adopted elements of the direct-to-consumer and surprise-release strategies that *In Rainbows* popularized. The album's approach prefigured the subscription and streaming models that would come to dominate music economics: by making price flexible, Radiohead implicitly acknowledged that the marginal cost of a digital copy was zero, anticipating the logic of streaming royalties. The experiment also revealed how powerfully audience loyalty could substitute for traditional marketing, a lesson that shaped artist strategies throughout the following decade.

The 2007 digital release arrived just weeks after [Amazon launched the Kindle]({{ '/news/science-technology/amazon-kindle-launch/' | relative_url }}), another instance of traditional media distribution being disrupted by direct digital delivery. Both events illustrated how rapidly legacy distribution infrastructure — record labels, brick-and-mortar bookstores — was being disintermediated as broadband internet became near-universal in wealthy countries.
