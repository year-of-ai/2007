## Summary

The **2007 MTV Video Music Awards** took place on September 9, 2007, at The Palms Casino Resort in Las Vegas, Nevada—a deliberate pivot from the traditional large-venue format of prior years, most recently Madison Square Garden in 2006. Hosted by comedian Sarah Silverman, whose opening remarks about Britney Spears's children drew immediate controversy, the ceremony was designed as MTV's smallest VMA ever—with 13 award categories eliminated and performances scattered across multiple hotel suites rather than a single stage. Despite the scaled-down ambitions, the event became a cultural touchstone for Britney Spears's controversial opening performance, Kanye West's public rift with the network, and the viral "Leave Britney Alone!" response video that defined internet culture of the moment. The ceremony drew approximately 5.2 million viewers on its first telecast, a fraction of the peak VMA audiences of the 1990s.

## Significance

### Britney Spears's Defining Low Point

Britney Spears opened the ceremony with a performance of "Gimme More," the lead single from her album *Blackout*, just six months after her highly publicized shaving of her head at a Tarzana, California, hair salon on February 16, 2007—an event that had already become the defining image of her public mental health crisis. The performance came amid a turbulent period that also included a custody dispute over her children with ex-husband Kevin Federline and a 5150 involuntary psychiatric hold in January 2008 that was still months away. Critics and viewers globally panned the performance as "one of the worst to grace the MTV Awards," with the *Los Angeles Times* noting she "teetered through her dance steps." Host Sarah Silverman followed with an opening joke—"Britney Spears, everyone. She is amazing. She is 25 years old and she's already accomplished everything she's going to accomplish in her life." The performance was later widely revisited as a moment of media scrutiny of a performer in visible distress. Her *Blackout* album, released October 30, 2007, would be critically rehabilitated over the following decade as a pioneering electronic pop record, reaching a Metacritic score of 79.

### "Leave Britney Alone!" — A Viral Internet Phenomenon

The performance sparked an immediate backlash that crystallized in the "Leave Britney Alone!" response video, released within hours. The viral clip accumulated over 2 million views within 24 hours, becoming a defining internet meme of 2007. The video's emotional outcry resonated with audiences concerned about Britney's public breakdown and the media's relentless scrutiny of her personal crisis. It marked a shift in how internet audiences engaged with celebrity discourse, capturing emerging digital activism and empathy.

### Kanye West's Public Break with MTV

During the ceremony, Kanye West was assigned to perform in a hotel suite rather than on the main stage, a scheduling decision that infuriated the artist. West publicly declared he would "never return to MTV," marking a significant rift between one of the music industry's most prominent figures and the network. His complaint highlighted the ceremony's diminished prestige and production scale, contributing to broader criticism of MTV's declining cultural centrality as the music video's dominance in the industry waned.

### The Smallest VMAs Ever

The 2007 ceremony represented MTV's most scaled-down VMA production to date, with 13 awards eliminated and performances scattered across multiple hotel suites rather than a traditional stage setup. The venue constraints and reduced scope drew criticism from artists and highlighted how the internet and YouTube (which launched an official presence in 2007) were reshaping how music videos reached audiences, undermining MTV's monopoly on music-video distribution and cultural authority.

### Awards: Key Winners

The 2007 VMAs distributed 11 awards (down from 24 in 2006). The major results:

| Award | Winner |
|---|---|
| Video of the Year | Rihanna ft. Jay-Z — "Umbrella" |
| Best Male Video | Justin Timberlake — "What Goes Around...Comes Around" |
| Best Female Video | Beyoncé — "Irreplaceable" |
| Best Hip-Hop Video | Kanye West — "Stronger" |
| Best Pop Video | Justin Timberlake — "What Goes Around...Comes Around" |
| Quadruple winner (overall) | Justin Timberlake (4 awards) |

Rihanna's Video of the Year win for "Umbrella"—a song that had spent ten weeks at No. 1 on the *Billboard* Hot 100 and 61 days atop the UK Singles Chart—marked her arrival as a global pop force. The track's title motif became one of 2007's most ubiquitous cultural references. Ironically, Kanye West's "Stronger" won Best Hip-Hop Video just days before the September 11 release of his album *Graduation*, whose chart-battle with 50 Cent's *Curtis* dominated music news that week.

### Other Notable Moments

- An altercation between Kid Rock and Tommy Lee erupted during the ceremony, resulting in both being escorted from the venue by security, adding to the event's chaotic narrative.
- Sarah Silverman's pointed hosting style—commenting on Paris Hilton's post-jail appearance and mocking Spears—drew a mixed response, with some critics praising her irreverence and others finding her remarks cruel given Spears's visible struggles.
- The decision to hold performances in separate Palms hotel suites (rather than a main stage) was framed as an "innovative" format but was widely read as a cost-cutting and logistical workaround given the non-arena venue.

### Aftermath: Britney's Conservatorship and the Media Reckoning

The 2007 VMAs performance is widely cited as the public nadir of Britney Spears's crisis era. On February 1, 2008—less than five months later—her father Jamie Spears was granted a temporary conservatorship over her person and estate by a Los Angeles court, a legal arrangement that would remain in place for 13 years until its termination in November 2021. The documentary *Framing Britney Spears* (FX/Hulu, 2021) revisited the 2007 VMA performance as a moment of systemic media failure, citing the relentless tabloid coverage and commentary—including Silverman's jokes—as evidence of how celebrity media exploited a person in crisis. The "Leave Britney Alone!" video's creator, Chris Crocker, became one of the earliest examples of a viral internet celebrity, later speaking about the emotional authenticity behind the video and the harassment they received in the years that followed.
