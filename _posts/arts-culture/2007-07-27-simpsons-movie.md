## Summary

**The Simpsons Movie** premiered on July 21, 2007, in Springfield, Vermont, with a wide theatrical release across North America on July 27, 2007. Directed by David Silverman, the film marked the first major theatrical adaptation of the long-running Fox animated series—18 years in the making after series creator Matt Groening first discussed a film in 1989. The script, developed over four years by a team of eleven writers including Al Jean, James L. Brooks, and Mike Scully, went through 100-plus drafts before reaching the screen. The film delivers the core narrative conceit that had sustained the show: Homer Simpson's bumbling endangers Springfield, requiring family redemption to save the town. Its commercial and critical success—$536.4 million worldwide on a $75 million budget—affirmed *The Simpsons*' place in American popular culture nearly two decades into its run.

## Significance

### Production: 18 Years in the Making

The production history of *The Simpsons Movie* is one of Hollywood's longest gestation periods for a theatrical adaptation. Matt Groening and James L. Brooks first floated a film concept in 1989, the same year the series debuted. Over the following decade and a half, multiple scripts were attempted and abandoned, as each new idea was eventually folded into the television series. The final screenplay—officially credited to Groening, Brooks, Al Jean, Mike Scully, and David Silverman among eleven total writers—reportedly went through over 100 drafts between 2001 and 2006. Principal production required re-recording dialogue from the core cast (Dan Castellaneta, Julie Kavner, Nancy Cartwright, Yeardley Smith, Hank Azaria, Harry Shearer), augmented by guest performances from Albert Brooks (as EPA administrator Russ Cargill) and Green Day (performing a rock version of the theme). The animation was produced at Film Roman and other facilities, with scenes rendered at twice the television series' visual resolution to support theatrical widescreen presentation.

### Record-Breaking Commercial Success

The Simpsons Movie opened with $74 million domestically in its opening weekend, setting a record for the largest opening weekend for an animated television adaptation and the largest non-sequel opening for an animated film at the time. The film went on to earn $183 million domestically and $536.4 million globally, making it the eighth-highest-grossing film of 2007. Its international performance was particularly strong, with $27.8 million from the United Kingdom (the second-highest opening for a Fox film there), $13.2 million from Australia (the highest animated-film opening in that territory), and strong returns across 71 overseas territories. The film recouped its $75 million production budget in its first domestic weekend alone.

### Cultural Phenomenon — The Kwik-E-Mart Campaign

The film's marketing campaign became a cultural event unto itself. 20th Century Fox partnered with 7-Eleven to transform approximately a dozen locations into "Kwik-E-Marts" themed after the fictional convenience store from the series. The promotion drove a 30% increase in profits at participating stores and captured the public's nostalgia for the series while attracting both longtime fans and those who had drifted from the show during its 2000s downturn. The SimpsonizeMe web promotion allowed fans to create personalized Simpsons avatars, further extending the film's cultural reach into early social media engagement.

### Critical Reception and Awards Recognition

The film earned an 87% "Certified Fresh" rating on Rotten Tomatoes based on 213 reviews, with a critics consensus praising it as a "feature-length episode" that "delivers the laughs." Roger Ebert gave it 3 out of 4 stars, writing that the film was "funnier than most of what passes for comedy in theaters today." It received a Golden Globe nomination for Best Animated Feature Film, competing against *Persepolis* and *Surf's Up*, and earned a BAFTA nomination for Best Animated Film—ultimately losing to *Ratatouille*. The film proved that the franchise could sustain a theatrical narrative beyond episodic television, even as the show itself had entered a period of diminishing critical enthusiasm with audiences debating the relative quality of post-season-10 episodes.

### The Franchise's Enduring Power

At a time when television-to-film adaptations were frequently dismissed as cash-grabs, *The Simpsons Movie* demonstrated that a beloved, long-running series could translate its cultural capital into a genuine theatrical event. The film was released during the height of streaming's emergence and the fragmentation of television audiences, yet it managed to unite millions of viewers around a theatrical experience—a feat that anticipated how franchises would increasingly rely on episodic television to fuel theatrical releases.

### Plot: Environmental Crisis as Narrative Conceit

Homer's careless dumping of pig waste into Lake Springfield triggers an EPA response that seals the town under a massive glass dome, forcing the Simpson family to flee to Alaska. The narrative structure mirrors the show's central dynamic: Homer's incompetence creates a town-wide crisis that only family unity can resolve. The environmental catastrophe plot reflected growing 2007 anxieties about climate change and ecological collapse, fitting the moment when *An Inconvenient Truth* had recently popularized climate discourse and the Nobel Peace Prize went to Al Gore and the IPCC.

### Box Office Context

Releasing in late July, the film competed with other summer blockbusters (Transformers had opened on July 3, Ratatouille on June 29). Yet its opening weekend performance demonstrated that animated films and franchise properties could claim significant market share even in a crowded release calendar—a lesson that would shape the film industry's blockbuster strategy for the coming decade.

### Legacy: Animation's Long Tail

*The Simpsons Movie* helped establish the viability of long-dormant television franchises as theatrical events. Its success preceded a wave of television-to-film projects (*South Park* had demonstrated this in 1999; subsequent successes include *The SpongeBob Movie* franchise and the eventual *Bob's Burgers Movie* in 2022). As of 2007, *The Simpsons* had aired around 400 episodes over 18 seasons; the film's success helped renew the series for additional seasons, contributing to a run that would eventually surpass 700 episodes.
