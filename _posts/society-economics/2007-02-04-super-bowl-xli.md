## Summary

On February 4, 2007, the Indianapolis Colts defeated the Chicago Bears 29-17 in Super Bowl XLI at Dolphin Stadium in Miami Gardens, Florida, before an announced attendance of 74,512 and a television audience of approximately 93.2 million viewers in the United States—the most-watched Super Bowl at the time of broadcast. The game was the culmination of the 2006 NFL season, pitting the AFC champion Colts (12-4 regular season) against the NFC champion Bears (13-3 regular season).

From the opening kickoff, the game produced history. Chicago return specialist Devin Hester fielded the opening kick and returned it 92 yards for a touchdown after just 14 seconds of play, the fastest score in Super Bowl history at the time. The Bears led 7-6 after a Colts fumble and a subsequent one-yard touchdown run gave them an early advantage. Indianapolis, however, steadied under Peyton Manning's direction and took the lead for good in the second quarter. The Colts ultimately won by converting turnovers into scoring opportunities: they forced Rex Grossman into two interceptions and recovered key fumbles in the final quarter to put the game beyond reach.

Quarterback Peyton Manning, who had carried a reputation for playoff underperformance despite his regular-season excellence, delivered the composed championship performance his critics had doubted he possessed. Manning completed 25 of 38 pass attempts for 247 yards and one touchdown, with one interception, earning Super Bowl MVP honors. Running back Dominic Rhodes contributed 113 yards on 21 carries, and the Colts' defense forced five turnovers over the course of the game.

The game was played under steady rainfall from the second quarter onward—the first Super Bowl contested in significant rain—which compromised the footing of skill players and contributed to both teams' unusually high turnover rates. The Colts and Bears combined for eight turnovers—five by Chicago and three by Indianapolis—among the highest totals in Super Bowl history.

## Historic Milestones

The contest produced three historically significant milestones that elevated it beyond an ordinary championship game:

**Tony Dungy's milestone:** By directing the Colts to victory, head coach Tony Dungy became the first African-American head coach in NFL history to win a Super Bowl. Lovie Smith, on the opposing sideline, had become only the second African-American head coach to reach the Super Bowl that season—meaning the game was the first in which both competing coaches were African American. Dungy's achievement drew recognition far beyond football, as it came amid ongoing discussion about the NFL's "Rooney Rule" (adopted in 2003), which required teams to interview minority candidates for head coaching vacancies.

**Indianapolis as a championship city:** The Colts' victory was the first major professional sports championship—across the NFL, NBA, MLB, and NHL—ever secured by a team based in Indianapolis. The franchise had relocated controversially from Baltimore in March 1984; the championship vindicated more than two decades of civic investment in the team and the construction of the RCA Dome. The city later used the championship momentum to bid successfully for Super Bowl XLVI, which it hosted in 2012.

**Manning's vindication:** Manning had been selected first overall in the 1998 draft with extraordinary expectations; his regular-season statistical record was historic, but the Colts' repeated playoff disappointments between 2000 and 2005 fueled skepticism about his ability to win under postseason pressure. The Super Bowl MVP award, combined with a 38-34 AFC Championship Game comeback against the New England Patriots one week earlier, fundamentally reshaped Manning's legacy.

## Broadcast and Cultural Impact

Super Bowl XLI was broadcast in the United States on CBS, with play-by-play announcer Jim Nantz and analyst Phil Simms. The halftime show featured Prince, who performed in the rain in what reviewers widely considered one of the greatest halftime performances in the event's history. Prince's set, including a rain-soaked rendition of "Purple Rain," drew an estimated half-time television audience of 86 million viewers.

The game generated approximately $185 million in economic activity for the Miami metropolitan area. Ticket prices on the secondary market averaged $4,200, a significant premium over the $600 face value of the highest-priced tickets. The broadcast achieved a 42.6 rating and a 64 share, meaning roughly two-thirds of televisions in use at game time were tuned to the broadcast.

## Significance

Super Bowl XLI's significance extended far beyond sport. For Indianapolis, it represented the culmination of a two-decade civic investment in the franchise since its controversial relocation from Baltimore; the championship validated the city's model of building civic identity around professional sports. For the NFL, the game underscored the league's growing demographic reach and its status as the dominant American sports entertainment property, drawing its largest television audience to date. Tony Dungy's historic victory accelerated a broader conversation about representation in professional sports leadership that continued throughout the decade, with the number of Black head coaches in the NFL increasing in subsequent years.
